The team should not proceed with deploying any of the three inference engines immediately. While the current benchmark provides valuable data on raw throughput and latency, it fails to account for the critical operational realities of a mobile device environment, specifically regarding session reuse, thermal dynamics, and artifact integrity. The primary recommendation is to halt the deployment cycle and instead conduct a targeted validation campaign focused on the "long session" scenario, which is the only viable use case for the current hardware constraints.

The most significant tradeoff lies between raw speed and session reliability. Engine Cedar offers the fastest prompt processing but suffers from slow decoding, which will cause severe latency spikes during the critical twenty-to-forty-minute window. Engine Rowan provides the fastest prompt processing but consumes excessive memory, risking OOM (Out of Memory) conditions on the limited device. Engine Juniper offers a balanced approach with steady generation but slower setup, which is acceptable for long sessions but unacceptable for the frequent one-request visits. Consequently, no single engine can be deemed "best" without a specific workload profile; Cedar is only viable if the device is managed to avoid thermal throttling, and Juniper is only viable if the session is guaranteed to be reused.

To ensure the recommendation is actionable, the team must strictly separate the following measurements:
1.  **Setup and Initialization Costs:** These must be measured separately from generation to accurately reflect the "expensive setup" mentioned in the brief. A route that appears fast in a benchmark but has a high setup cost will fail in production.
2.  **Thermal and Power Dynamics:** The benchmark assumes constant conditions, but the device's clock rates and power draw change with temperature and battery state. Measurements must include the battery and thermal state *before* and *after* the campaign to account for these variables.
3.  **Artifact Integrity:** The benchmark must verify that outputs are sensible text. Broken routes that emit binary fragments or short loops must be flagged as non-functional, regardless of their raw token count.
4.  **Provenance and Source:** Since artifacts come from different sources (original checkpoints, community conversions, quantized packages), the report must explicitly list the source revision, file hashes, and quantization method for every route to ensure reproducibility.

The final recommendation should be to prioritize the engine that offers the best balance between setup time and generation speed *only* if the device is confirmed to be stable and the session is reused. If the device is shared with development work, the benchmark must be re-run under an exclusive device lock to prevent stale remote directories from invalidating resource observations. Furthermore, the report must explicitly state that the benchmark does not guarantee narrative quality or factual accuracy, as these are evaluated in a separate phase.
