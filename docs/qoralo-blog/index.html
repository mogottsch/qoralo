Building a NotebookLM-like podcast on my phone

NotebookLM's Audio Overviews turn your sources into a conversation between AI hosts. They impress me, even when the result gives me AI-slop vibes rather than feeling like a real podcast.

How close could we get with all inference running on a phone?

Two models, one phone

On my Galaxy S23 Ultra, Gemma writes scripts on the GPU; Supertonic makes speech on the NPU. The CPU handles orchestration. For now, mine writes a script and reads it in one voice. Model preparation and compilation happen on a computer.

Pocket reuses attention keys and values, its KV cache, at each autoregressive step. Identical inputs didn't guarantee identical runtime behavior. The first stop prediction already differed, before the first sampled audio latent. We replaced that path with a fixed 256-slot cache, explicit positions and a mask for valid entries.

Test complete generation, not just graph execution. State updates, stopping behavior and listening all belong in a port's acceptance test.

Then, a whole chapter

Later, with Supertonic, a 487-word input produced about 30 seconds of unusable audio on both CPU and HTP. After following Supertonic's full recipe, the new CPU and HTP recordings sounded good.

The same chapter was split into 15 pieces, synthesized separately and joined. Generation settings changed too. Port the text preparation and synthesis loop too, not just the neural graphs.

These are standalone experiments, not a NotebookLM quality match. The app is called Qoralo; its final UI-to-audio demo and listening review are still pending.

Technical details & experiment records

Pocket TTS: state and runtime parity

The August 20 Pocket clips are both Android CPU, fixed Alba voice, temperature 0.3, same short text and seed 0. They predate the later Supertonic pipeline. The original Android dynamic graph's first AR end-of-speech logit differs from the host after identical text tokens and initial Alba caches, before a sampled latent can feed back. The 0.3862 logit difference is a diagnostic observation, not a probability difference or an established cause of the audible failure.

The replacement has twelve right-aligned KV tensors (six layers, keys and values), explicit absolute positions and an additive validity mask. Its 256 slots retain 126 voice + 26 text + up to 100 generated positions = 252, without rolling away voice context. The diagram's recovery lane is a different graph, not a claim that the original graph became correct unchanged. Audio lengths include decoder tail frames; EOS step 53 and 56 emitted latent frames are therefore not interchangeable counts. The broader per-operator cause was not proved.

The rejected original CPU WAV is SHA-256 cdf77f6ecaecdbb1a95992326675972366b57da864864f7359890b7e2df5c4b6. The accepted fixed-cache CPU WAV is e0638f35870b24fbe675649a0cd49fa69ca6d74060557bfec5336d2d03a21c9a. These are byte-identical retained recordings, not recreated demos. The fixed-cache HTP route was accepted separately; neither player here is an HTP sample. No voice-cloning claim follows.

Supertonic: whole-input versus full recipe

The chapter chart compares CPU output from experiments 0036 and 0037 on the same text. Widths use actual sample counts at 44,100 Hz. The corresponding 0037 HTP recording was also accepted in listening. Acceptance belongs to those experimental files, not an unreviewed app recording. About 30 seconds was observed in the failed attempt, not established as a hard model cap.

The recipe groups raw sentences toward 300 code points before normalization, keeps individually overlong sentences intact, uses English/M1, eight steps and speed 1.05, and joins full native waveforms with 0.3-second gaps. Several settings changed together; this does not isolate segmentation as the cause. The earlier CPU/HTP waveform comparison and the later HTP source numerical comparison failed their thresholds. Listening does not turn those into numerical passes.

Preparation and placement

Gemma uses LiteRT-LM's GPU route; Supertonic uses ONNX Runtime + QNN's HTP backend. CPU work remains. The FP32 speech source graphs do not imply FP32 accelerator arithmetic: the pinned runtime requested FP16; per-operation precision was not measured. Models and compiled contexts were prepared on a computer, not trained or compiled entirely on the phone.

Precompilation also reduced median construction of four QNN sessions from 21.6129 s to 0.497816 s: experiment 0023, fixed T239/L238 input, three fresh processes per method. Those measurements exclude generation and offline compilation. They are not app-launch or current episode-latency measurements.
