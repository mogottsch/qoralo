Qoralo, on my phone

I'm building Qoralo, an Android app for turning source material into podcasts. The models run on my Galaxy S23 Ultra.

Where the work runs

Gemma writes the script on the GPU. Supertonic makes the speech on the phone's NPU. The CPU runs the app and prepares the inputs.

Preparing the
speech models

We compiled the speech models ahead of time, on a computer. The phone then loaded the prepared files.

A whole chapter
came out wrong

Short clips worked. A 487-word chapter produced about 30 seconds of unusable audio on both CPU and HTP.

We used Supertonic's recipe on the same chapter: 15 shorter pieces, processed separately and joined with short gaps. We changed the generation settings too. I listened to the new CPU and HTP recordings. Both sounded good.

Coding agents wrote much of the code. I had to reconstruct the steps to understand what we'd built.

I still need to run the final app demo and listen to its output.

Notes & experiment records

The charts use retained experiments, not measurements of a new app build. The setup comparison is experiment 0023's fixed T239/L238 input. It measures construction of four QNN sessions, excluding speech generation, offline preparation and compilation. It is not an app-launch measurement or a claim that speech became 43 times faster.

The chapter chart compares CPU output from experiments 0036 and 0037 on the same text. Segment widths use actual sample counts at 44,100 Hz. The corresponding 0037 HTP recording was also accepted in listening. That acceptance belongs to those experimental files; it does not transfer to an unreviewed app recording. About 30 seconds was observed in the failed attempt, not established as a hard model cap.

The provider recipe groups raw sentences toward 300 code points before normalization, keeps individually overlong sentences intact, uses English/M1, eight steps and speed 1.05, and joins full native waveforms with 0.3-second gaps. Several settings changed together, so this does not isolate segmentation as the cause. The earlier CPU/HTP waveform comparison and the later HTP source numerical comparison failed their respective thresholds. Listening results do not turn them into numerical passes.

Gemma uses LiteRT-LM's GPU route; Supertonic uses ONNX Runtime with QNN's HTP backend. CPU work remains. The FP32 speech source graphs do not imply FP32 accelerator arithmetic: the pinned runtime requested FP16. Per-operation precision was not measured. Recent headless tests completed multi-chapter scripts through the production queue; those are separate from the final UI-to-audio demo still to be done.
