Before the street grid

Every city carries traces of the water that shaped it. Long before roads fixed our routes, streams ran through the low ground. As buildings spread, some of those streams were diverted into underground channels. The water disappeared from view, but the shape of the landscape remained.

During a heavy storm, rain falls on roofs and paved streets instead of soaking into soil. It reaches drains quickly. If more water arrives than those drains can carry, it collects in low places. Some of those places follow the paths of older waterways. That helps explain why the same intersections can flood again, even when the stream beneath them is no longer visible.

Opening a buried stream is one possible response. An open channel and nearby green space can give floodwater more room and provide habitat for wildlife. But restoration needs land, funding, and careful design. It cannot solve every drainage problem. Understanding the old landscape is a useful starting point, not a guarantee that the next storm will cause no damage.
